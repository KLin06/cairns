Often referred to as one of the most beautiful and most challenging day hikes in Ontario, The Crack travels through mixed forest lowlands and climbs the white cliffs of the La Cloche Mountains to end in panoramic views of Killarney’s many lakes. The Crack’s rugged terrain, long distance, and steep incline make this trail difficult, even for experience hikers. Be prepared for sharp rocks that get slippery when wet, large and small cobble, crumbly soil, exposed roots, and larger boulders. This is a trail that often takes all four limbs in its many scrambles. Despite being extremely steep, going up is often easier than coming back down, and good balance is necessary to complete the trail safely. The Crack is an out-and-back trail. When you reach the summit , remember to turn around and return the way you came, following the red trail markers the entire way. A portion of The Crack overlaps with the La Cloche Silhouette Trail, an 80km backpacking loop, which is marked with blue trail markers. Proper preparation is essential. Remember to bring: • 2 liters of water per hiker • Snacks for each hiker • Insect repellent and sunscreen • Sturdy hiking shoes and weather appropriate clothing • A first aid kit • A map and compass or GPS device (don’t rely on your cellphone for navigation) • A satellite communications device or cellphone for use in an emergency (note that cellphone service is limited along the trail) A Daily Vehicle Permit is required to park at the trailhead, which can be pre-booked up to five days in advance of your arrival. If you’re camping in Killarney Provincial Park, visit the park office to reserve your parking space. The Crack is not a suitable trail for children under the age of seven or those without experience hiking in rough terrain. Pets may also find the rock scrambles and length of the trail challenging. Leave no trace – pack out all garbage, don’t bring cans or glass bottles into the backcountry, and stay on the trail. Camping and campfires are not permitted anywhere on The Crack trail. When hiking in winter, check the weather and plan accordingly. The park regularly posts the snow depth on the Ontario Parks Snow Report. You may need snowshoes and microspikes/ice cleats to complete the trail safely. Snowshoes can be rented from the Friends of Killarney Park. Your hike will take longer in the snow. Plan to complete the trail well before sunset.

Trailgoers are saying

The Crack Trail is recognized for its stunning scenic views and is often described as a dog-friendly trail. Reviewers appreciate that the initial sections are suitable for hiking with dogs, but caution that the last part is a rock scramble, which can be challenging for pets. Families will find it rewarding, as many comments highlight the beautiful views at the summit, making it a worthwhile endeavor for all ages.

Here are some of the most recent reviews:

  • "Dog-friendly right up to the rock scramble to get to the crack viewpoint. Very difficult to get a dog up there unless you carry them."
  • "The view at the end is so worth it!"
  • "Great hike! Beautiful view at the top! The rocky part is hard for dogs, ours needed some help."

Despite the trail's beauty, several challenges are noted. The terrain becomes quite rocky and steep, especially near the summit, which may require climbing for some. Reviewers recommend bringing bug spray and plenty of water as insects can be bothersome, particularly in the warmer months. Also, due to the rocky sections, it's suggested to be cautious with dogs, as they may need assistance navigating the final climb.
